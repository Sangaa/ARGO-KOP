# HORUS Session Record 006 — 2026-08-23

## Objective
Continue HORUS under truth-before-theory and HERMUZ-style construction discipline. Strengthen the analytical method by separating three questions that are often conflated: whether an event occurred, whether ARGO caused/selected the behavior, and whether the behavior represents a reusable capability.

## Evidence boundary
No new autonomy capability is promoted in this session. The session extends the measurement and attribution framework and records how future historical evidence must be classified.

## Decisions

### DEC-H028 — Event, agency, and capability are separate axes
A future record must not collapse `behavior observed`, `behavior selected/caused by ARGO`, and `capability demonstrated`. Each requires its own evidence.

### DEC-H029 — Counterfactual strength is graded
Historical evidence may support different counterfactual strengths. HORUS must label whether a counterfactual is directly tested, naturally occurring, reconstructed, or unavailable rather than pretending all historical cases are equally causal.

### DEC-H030 — Retention is not transfer
A strategy reused in the same domain demonstrates persistence; reuse in a materially different context is needed before calling it transfer.

### DEC-H031 — Transfer is not independent discovery
A strategy may transfer because it was explicitly taught, retrieved, or embedded in prior experience. Transfer evidence therefore does not by itself prove independent discovery.

### DEC-H032 — The strongest autonomy claim requires converging evidence
No single signal should establish autonomous learning. Stronger claims require convergence across attribution, persistence, changed-context performance, and bounded alternative explanations.

### DEC-H033 — Preserve the weakest defensible claim
When evidence supports several interpretations, record the strongest interpretation as a hypothesis but make the weakest defensible interpretation the factual conclusion.

## Analytical findings

### Finding F-H006-01 — The autonomy ladder
HORUS now distinguishes an evidence ladder:

`Observed Behavior → Adaptation → Retained Strategy → Contextual Transfer → Independent Strategy Selection → Strategy Improvement → Meta-Learning`

Higher levels require evidence beyond the lower levels. A lower-level observation cannot be promoted merely because the higher-level interpretation is plausible.

### Finding F-H006-02 — Agency attribution is the bottleneck
For the current ARGO investigation, the difficult question is increasingly not whether a strategy change happened, but whether the available evidence can attribute the choice of that strategy to ARGO rather than to protocol, retrieval, task structure, or evaluator influence.

### Finding F-H006-03 — Counterfactuals increase causal strength
Evidence becomes more causally informative when there is a meaningful comparison showing that the behavior would likely differ without the relevant experience, instruction, or triggering condition. Absence of such a comparison lowers causal confidence; it does not automatically falsify the behavior.

### Finding F-H006-04 — Historical evidence has an asymmetry
Historical records can strongly establish what was recorded, but may be weak for establishing what alternatives were available to ARGO at the moment of choice. Therefore historical meta-learning claims require explicit accounting of missing context.

### Finding F-H006-05 — Capability claims require boundary conditions
A capability statement must specify the domain, task family, context changes, prerequisites, and evidence limits. `ARGO can do X` is incomplete without stating under what conditions.

## Evidence classification template

For every candidate historical case:

| Axis | Required question |
|---|---|
| Event | What behavior actually occurred? |
| Outcome | What changed in the observable result? |
| Agency | What evidence attributes the change to ARGO? |
| Alternatives | What other causes remain viable? |
| Strategy | What method was used before and after? |
| Diagnosis | Is there evidence ARGO identified the limitation? |
| Persistence | Was the change retained? |
| Transfer | Did it reappear in a materially changed context? |
| Counterfactual | What comparison supports causal inference? |
| Boundary | Where does the evidence stop supporting the claim? |
| Status | What epistemic state is justified? |

## New falsification questions

Future cases should actively ask:

1. Could the same behavior have occurred if ARGO had not learned from the triggering experience?
2. Was the strategy already present in retrieved experience?
3. Did the protocol or task wording effectively specify the strategy?
4. Did evaluator feedback reveal the strategy?
5. Is the apparent transfer merely a repeated template?
6. Does the strategy persist after the original context disappears?
7. Does a changed context require the underlying principle rather than surface imitation?

## Handoff lesson for ARGO/HERMUZ

Reusable lesson:

> **A stronger claim requires stronger attribution, not merely more examples.**

More repetitions can increase confidence that a behavior occurs, while still adding little information about who or what caused it.
