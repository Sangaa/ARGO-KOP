## E0–E4 assessment

### E0 — Executed: SUPPORTED
The tested implementation executed and produced the documented behavior. The evidence chain is explicit: source claim → implementation → observed output → expected/unexpected comparison → test evidence.

### E1 — Available: SUPPORTED WITH NARROW SCOPE
The tested strategy/concept was available in the controlled fixture, and provenance to the implementation and tests is preserved. This establishes availability within the experiment. It does not establish independent origin by ARGO.

### E2 — Selected: NOT ESTABLISHED
The record does not establish that ARGO compared multiple meaningful alternatives at a decision boundary and independently selected the `add(a,b)` strategy. The presence of an implementation and its test is compatible with externally supplied construction.

### E3 — Abstracted: NOT ESTABLISHED

### E4 — Mechanistically supported: NOT ESTABLISHED

## Critical finding

### F-H019-01 — A rigorously controlled learning record can still be weak evidence about learner agency
This candidate is strong as evidence that a narrow programming behavior was tested and bounded correctly. It is weak as evidence about who originated or selected the strategy. Experimental rigor about the artifact does not automatically become evidence about the agency of the learner.

### F-H019-02 — Promotion discipline is itself evidence of epistemic maturity, not autonomous learning
The promoted record explicitly preserves scope, authority, validation, confidence, and provenance. This demonstrates disciplined knowledge governance. It does not demonstrate that ARGO autonomously discovered the promoted rule.

### F-H019-03 — The repository already contains a useful anti-overclaim pattern
The evidence record states that the specific tested concepts may be promoted while rejecting the universal statement that every function should have a clear responsibility. HORUS recognizes this as a correct separation between local evidence and universal generalization.

### F-H019-04 — E0/E1 can be strong while E2–E4 remain empty
This is not an inconsistency. It is the expected result when the artifact is well tested but the learner's decision process, transfer behavior, and causal predictions were not instrumented.

### F-H019-05 — The first candidate exposes a category error to avoid
A `promoted learning record` is not automatically a `record of autonomous learning`. Promotion answers whether a claim is sufficiently supported for its declared scope; it does not answer who generated the claim or how the strategy originated.

## What would be required to move E2 upward

A new or historical case would need, at minimum:

1. A defined decision boundary.
2. More than one meaningful candidate strategy or an explicit strategy space.
3. Evidence that the alternatives were available/representable.
4. Evidence that no external actor directly imposed the selected strategy.
5. A trace sufficient to attribute the choice to ARGO.

## What would be required to move E3 upward

The selected principle must be tested under meaningful surface variation while preserving the relevant causal structure. Success must be distinguishable from memorized or superficial pattern reuse.

## What would be required to move E4 upward

ARGO would need to generate predictions tied to a causal factor, undergo a controlled manipulation of that factor, and produce predictions that are independently validated against observed consequences. Retrospective explanation alone is insufficient.
