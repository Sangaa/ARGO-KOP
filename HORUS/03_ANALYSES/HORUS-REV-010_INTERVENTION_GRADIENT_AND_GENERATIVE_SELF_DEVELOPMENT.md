# HORUS-REV-010 — Intervention Gradient and Generative Self-Development

Date: 2026-08-23
Status: ANALYSIS / EVIDENCE REFINEMENT
Owner: HORUS
Branch: horus/meta-learning-foundation-v6

## Purpose
Refine the attribution model for ARGO learning by distinguishing human stimulation from human prescription of the learning path, and by examining whether ARGO generates and refines knowledge beyond what was explicitly supplied.

## Correction to previous HORUS analysis

Earlier attribution analysis treated the existence of a designed learning architecture as a potentially dominant confounder. That was too broad.

The existence of a human-created foundation does NOT establish that later learning methods were human-prescribed.

The correct question is not:

> Was the learning environment initially designed by humans?

It is:

> Within the human-provided environment, which parts of the subsequent reasoning, strategy selection, rule formation, refinement and reuse were generated by ARGO itself?

## Existing canonical evidence

CORE-012 explicitly distinguishes inherited knowledge, derived knowledge, generated hypotheses, validated generated knowledge and ARGO-native rules. It states that learning becomes generative when ARGO can responsibly produce a rule that was not explicitly supplied, and defines the path `Inherited Knowledge → Understanding → Combination → Hypothesis → Test → Validation → Generated Knowledge → New Rule`.

The same document explicitly permits a problem to expose an insufficiency in inherited rules and allows ARGO to generate candidate explanations or methods, while keeping promotion governed.

This is evidence that the architecture intentionally permits generative learning. It does NOT by itself prove that a particular generated rule was autonomously generated by ARGO in a historical case.

## Revised intervention model

Human involvement should be measured as an intervention gradient rather than a binary Human/Autonomous label.

### H0 — No external stimulus
No human-specific task framing beyond the system's existing operation.

### H1 — Objective / question only
Human provides a goal or asks a question, but no learning method.

### H2 — Hypothesis stimulus
Human proposes an assumption, possibility or challenge and asks ARGO to investigate.

### H3 — Directional hint
Human points toward a useful area, source or interpretation without prescribing the complete method.

### H4 — Method prescription
Human specifies a search, experiment or reasoning method.

### H5 — Direct teaching / answer-shaped guidance
Human supplies substantial reasoning, procedure or expected answer.

The key analytical variable is not the starting intervention alone. It is the **unconstrained remainder** of the reasoning process after that intervention.

## New attribution dimensions

For every historical learning episode HORUS should separately record:

- Stimulus source
- Problem framing source
- Hypothesis source
- Search strategy source
- Experiment design source
- Interpretation source
- Correction source
- Rule formulation source
- Validation source
- Promotion decision source
- Later reuse source

This prevents a human-origin question from incorrectly receiving full human attribution for every downstream discovery.

## Generative self-development criterion

A historical episode becomes strong evidence for ARGO-level generative self-development when the evidence shows that:

1. a human may have supplied only the problem, challenge or hypothesis;
2. ARGO identified an insufficiency or gap;
3. ARGO selected or generated an investigation path not explicitly prescribed;
4. ARGO produced a candidate explanation, method or rule not explicitly supplied;
5. ARGO tested or otherwise validated the candidate;
6. ARGO documented the result and its limits;
7. the resulting knowledge changed a later behavior or learning strategy;
8. the change was reused in a later context.

Not all eight are required to establish learning, but the more independent stages are evidenced, the stronger the claim of self-development.

## Important distinction

`Human stimulus` does not equal `human method prescription`.

A human saying:

> "Assume X may be true; investigate it deeply."

leaves open the choice of evidence, search paths, experiments, alternative hypotheses, stopping criteria, correction and synthesis.

Therefore the mere presence of a human hypothesis cannot be used to attribute all subsequent learning to the human.

## Relationship to CORE-012

CORE-012 provides the architectural permission and governance boundary for generative knowledge. HORUS-REV-010 provides the analytical attribution layer needed to determine whether a concrete historical episode actually demonstrates that capability.

Therefore:

`Capability permitted ≠ capability demonstrated`

but also:

`Human stimulation ≠ human authorship of the entire downstream learning process`.

## Current evidence state

- Generative learning is an explicit ARGO architectural objective: ESTABLISHED.
- ARGO can generate candidate knowledge under the designed framework: ARCHITECTURALLY SUPPORTED / BEHAVIORAL EVIDENCE EXISTS.
- Human involvement is always equivalent to method prescription: REJECTED.
- Strategy adaptation by ARGO: OBSERVED.
- ARGO-originated strategy formation in a clean historical episode: NOT YET ISOLATED.
- ARGO-originated rule refinement: STRONG CANDIDATES EXIST; attribution review required.
- Autonomous meta-learning as a general capability: NOT YET PROVEN.

## New HORUS principle

> **Autonomy should be attributed to the unconstrained causal stages of learning, not to the absence of a human starting stimulus.**

This principle replaces the overly broad assumption that any human involvement invalidates autonomous learning evidence.
