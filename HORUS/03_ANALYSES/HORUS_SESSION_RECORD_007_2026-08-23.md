# HORUS Session Record 007 — 2026-08-23

## Objective
Continue HORUS with truth-before-theory. The purpose of this session is to make the autonomy ladder operational by defining what evidence would actually justify movement between levels, while preserving uncertainty and preventing circular reasoning.

## Boundary
No capability is promoted in this session. This is an analytical measurement specification for future evidence review.

## Decisions

### DEC-H034 — Define promotion criteria before reviewing a candidate
HORUS must specify what would count as sufficient evidence before judging a historical case. Criteria must not be rewritten merely to rescue a preferred interpretation.

### DEC-H035 — Separate discovery evidence from competence evidence
A system can competently execute a strategy without discovering it. Evidence of competence therefore cannot be used as evidence of discovery unless the causal origin is independently supported.

### DEC-H036 — Avoid circular attribution
The statement "ARGO chose the strategy because ARGO learned" cannot be used as evidence that ARGO learned. Attribution must rely on observations independent of the conclusion being tested.

### DEC-H037 — Baseline comparison is mandatory where feasible
A strategy-change claim should compare behavior before and after the relevant experience or intervention, and should specify what counts as meaningful change.

### DEC-H038 — Generalization must be materially different
A new task counts as transfer evidence only when the relevant surface features change enough that copying the original template is an inadequate explanation.

### DEC-H039 — Discovery requires an origin gap
A candidate independent-discovery case must demonstrate that the strategy was not explicitly supplied, not trivially retrieved from known prior experience, and not mechanically implied by the task/protocol.

## Operational evidence ladder

### Level 1 — Observed behavior
Minimum evidence: reproducible observation or reliable historical record.

What it proves: the behavior occurred within the observed context.

Does not prove: learning, agency, or capability beyond that context.

### Level 2 — Adaptation
Minimum evidence: a meaningful behavioral change following a relevant experience or condition.

What it supports: adaptation associated with the condition.

Does not prove: that ARGO selected the adaptation mechanism.

### Level 3 — Retained strategy
Minimum evidence: the changed strategy remains after the triggering episode and is reused under sufficiently comparable conditions.

What it supports: persistence.

Does not prove: transfer or independent discovery.

### Level 4 — Contextual transfer
Minimum evidence: the strategy or underlying structure reappears in a materially changed context where surface imitation is insufficient.

What it supports: transfer of a strategy/structure.

Does not prove: independent origin.

### Level 5 — Independent strategy selection
Minimum evidence: multiple viable strategies existed; the selected strategy was not prescribed; the selection is attributable to ARGO through evidence not dependent on the conclusion itself; and alternative causes are bounded.

What it supports: candidate autonomous strategy selection.

### Level 6 — Strategy improvement
Minimum evidence: an existing strategy produced a limitation; ARGO identified or behaviorally responded to that limitation; a materially improved strategy emerged without being supplied; the improvement is demonstrated by outcome comparison.

What it supports: candidate autonomous strategy improvement.

### Level 7 — Meta-learning
Minimum evidence: strategy improvement is retained, transferred, and used to improve future learning itself across changed tasks, with attribution and confounders sufficiently bounded.

What it supports: candidate independent meta-learning.

## Falsification matrix

| Claim | Must be challenged by |
|---|---|
| Learning | Could the result be explained by direct instruction or retrieval? |
| Adaptation | Could the behavior be a transient response rather than a learned change? |
| Retention | Is the behavior actually persistent or merely repeated under identical conditions? |
| Transfer | Could surface similarity explain the result? |
| Independent selection | Was the choice indirectly prescribed by protocol, task, or feedback? |
| Improvement | Was the improved method supplied or retrieved rather than developed? |
| Meta-learning | Does the change improve learning strategy itself and persist across changed contexts? |

## New analytical findings

### F-H007-01 — Promotion criteria are part of the evidence system
Defining the threshold before evaluating a case reduces retrospective interpretation drift. This does not make the criteria objectively true; it makes the evaluation procedure more auditable.

### F-H007-02 — Discovery has a negative-evidence burden
To argue independent discovery, HORUS must actively rule out reasonable alternative origins. Positive behavior alone is insufficient.

### F-H007-03 — Capability is conditional
A capability statement should always include its operating boundary. General statements without task/context constraints are analytically weak.

### F-H007-04 — The absence of a control does not invalidate a historical case, but lowers attribution strength
Historical evidence can remain useful if its limitations are explicit. It should not be silently promoted to causal proof.

### F-H007-05 — The safest factual conclusion is often lower than the strongest hypothesis
HORUS may retain a strong hypothesis for investigation while recording a narrower factual conclusion. This is not indecision; it is epistemic separation.

## Candidate-case scoring scaffold

For future historical cases, HORUS may record qualitative states for:

`Observation Quality / Attribution Strength / Alternative-Cause Control / Baseline Quality / Retention / Transfer / Counterfactual Strength / Discovery Origin Gap / Replication`

These are assessment dimensions, not an automatic numeric truth score.
