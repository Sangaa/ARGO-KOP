## 6. Required semantic quality questions

For every candidate reusable experience, HORUS recommends evaluating at least:

1. **Claim** — what exactly is being asserted?
2. **Observed facts** — what was directly observed?
3. **Inference** — what interpretation was derived from those observations?
4. **Invariant** — what structural rule is proposed to transfer?
5. **Boundary** — where does the invariant stop applying?
6. **Counterexample / counterindication** — what evidence would weaken or defeat it?
7. **Failure lineage** — did the lesson emerge from a real failure, a test artifact, an infrastructure gap, or an assumption?
8. **Current applicability** — is the source still relevant to the current architecture?
9. **Evidence independence** — are multiple supporting records actually independent?
10. **Authority compatibility** — does the lesson advise within its scope or compete with current authority?
11. **Supersession state** — was a later lesson a refinement, contradiction, or replacement?
12. **Transfer evidence** — has the invariant succeeded outside its source surface?

This is the semantic-content problem previously described as examining the **متن** rather than only the chain of provenance.

## 7. HORUS semantic acceptance classes

HORUS proposes analytical classification only; these are not governance states:

### A — STRUCTURALLY SOUND / TRANSFER-CANDIDATE
The facts, inference, invariant and boundary are distinguishable; no unsupported authority claim; at least one meaningful transfer test is possible.

### B — USEFUL BUT BOUNDED
The lesson is valid in its source context but broad transfer is not justified.

### C — HISTORICALLY VALUABLE / CURRENTLY STALE
The record is important provenance but should not actively guide current work without explicit historical retrieval intent.

### D — CORRELATED SUPPORT ONLY
The lesson appears supported by multiple records, but the records share one evidence lineage and do not provide independent confirmation.

### E — SEMANTIC CONFLICT
Two lessons make mutually incompatible claims at the same relevant claim/scope/time layer. Requires current evidence review.

### F — OVER-GENERALIZED
A source observation was promoted into an invariant wider than evidence supports.

### G — CONTENT INSUFFICIENT
The record is too compressed or vague to recover facts, inference, boundary or failure meaning.

### H — AUTHORITY-CONTAMINATED
An advisory lesson implicitly behaves as policy/governance without valid authority.

These classes should remain analytical until HERMUZ verifies whether a governed representation is needed.

## 9. Relationship to IGT

Semantic auditing and cognitive-effect IGT answer different questions.

Semantic audit asks:

`IS THE RETRIEVED EXPERIENCE ITSELF WELL-FORMED AND CURRENTLY APPLICABLE?`

IGT asks:

`DOES PROVIDING THAT EXPERIENCE IMPROVE NOVEL-TASK REASONING UNDER CONTROLLED CONDITIONS?`

Both are required.

A positive IGT result over poor content could encode accidental shortcutting.

A perfect semantic audit without participant evidence would still not prove cognitive benefit.

Therefore the correct sequence is:

`SEMANTIC EXPERIENCE QUALITY → BOUNDED RETRIEVAL → INDEPENDENT IGT → PROMOTION DECISION`.
