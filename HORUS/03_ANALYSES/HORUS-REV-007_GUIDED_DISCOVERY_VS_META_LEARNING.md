## Purpose
Determine whether the current ARGO evidence demonstrates autonomous improvement of the learning process, rather than successful use of a learning method that was already defined or guided.

## Critical finding
MEM-008 is unusually important because it explicitly distinguishes direct instruction, hints, independent inference, prior-experience use, self-correction and novel behavior, and defines G0-G4 guidance levels. It therefore gives HORUS a measurement vocabulary for separating guided learning from autonomous discovery.

However, the existence of the method does not prove that ARGO independently invented the method. The documented historical method was itself a designed training procedure. Therefore successful performance under MEM-008 is evidence of learning capability, not by itself evidence of autonomous learning-strategy invention.

## Evidence ladder

| Behavior | Current evidence | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Follows taught rule | Strong | Rule application |
| Corrects after feedback | Strong | Guided self-correction |
| Uses prior experience | Strong | Retrieval / transfer |
| Predicts from prior model | Observed | Predictive application |
| Refines model after contradiction | Observed | Model refinement |
| Transfers to changed context | Some evidence | Guided or partially guided transfer |
| Independently chooses a better learning method | Not established | Meta-learning candidate only |
| Independently identifies that its learning process failed | Not established across novel tasks | Required for stronger meta-learning claim |
| Independently changes retrieval/testing strategy and improves outcome | Not established | Key discriminating evidence |

## New distinction

HORUS introduces a four-level interpretation for future observations:

1. **Method execution** — ARGO performs a known learning procedure.
2. **Method adaptation** — ARGO modifies how the known procedure is applied to fit a new situation.
3. **Method selection** — ARGO independently chooses among learning strategies because it predicts which will produce better evidence.
4. **Method invention/improvement** — ARGO identifies a weakness in its learning strategy, proposes a materially different strategy, tests it, and demonstrates improved learning performance.

Only levels 3-4 provide strong evidence toward autonomous meta-learning.

## Why this matters

A system can appear highly self-learning while actually executing a sophisticated externally designed learning protocol. That capability is valuable, but it is epistemically different from independently improving the protocol itself.

Therefore HORUS must never infer meta-learning merely from:
- successful self-correction;
- successful prior-learning retrieval;
- successful transfer;
- or successful prediction.

Those are prerequisites or components, not sufficient proof.

## Current ARGO assessment

The corpus strongly supports an evidence-governed learning architecture and several forms of guided discovery. It does not yet establish that ARGO independently modifies its own learning strategy.

This narrows the central research question from:

> Does ARGO learn?

to:

> Can ARGO detect that its current way of learning is inadequate, select or invent a better learning strategy, test that strategy, and retain the improvement as a reusable meta-level experience?

## Discriminating experiment design

A future experiment should provide:
- a novel task family;
- more than one plausible learning/search strategy;
- no instruction about which strategy to choose;
- measurable outcome quality;
- opportunity for failure;
- opportunity to inspect whether ARGO changes strategy after failure;
- a second task from the same family to test retention and transfer.

Record:

`Initial strategy → Evidence → Outcome → Diagnosis → Strategy change → Re-test → Outcome delta → Retention → Transfer`

The evaluator must not reveal the intended meta-learning hypothesis during the experiment.

## Truth boundary

Current state:

`STRUCTURED LEARNING: ESTABLISHED`
`GUIDED DISCOVERY: ESTABLISHED`
`MODEL REFINEMENT: OBSERVED`
`CROSS-DOMAIN TRANSFER: OBSERVED / PARTLY GUIDED`
`AUTONOMOUS STRATEGY SELECTION: UNPROVEN`
`AUTONOMOUS STRATEGY IMPROVEMENT: UNPROVEN`
`AUTONOMOUS META-LEARNING: UNPROVEN`

## HORUS conclusion

The correct claim is not that ARGO has already achieved autonomous meta-learning. The evidence supports a narrower and more useful claim: ARGO has a documented learning architecture capable of retrieval, guided discovery, prediction, correction, model refinement, provenance control and transfer. The next frontier is to determine whether ARGO can independently improve the process by which it acquires those capabilities.
