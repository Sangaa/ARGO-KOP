# HORUS Analytical Knowledge Baseline 001

**Purpose:** Consolidate the material analytical findings produced during the HORUS learning-analysis work into one traceable baseline.

## A. Established observations

### A1. ARGO demonstrates learning behavior
Evidence reviewed across the repository shows a governed pipeline in which observations can become learning candidates, undergo evidence checks and verification, and only then become promoted learning. This supports the conclusion that ARGO has a structured learning mechanism.

### A2. ARGO can integrate experience and refine models
Historical engineering and learning records show repeated use of prior experience, diagnosis of failures, refinement of models, and reuse of lessons. These are stronger than isolated successful outputs because they involve changes informed by prior evidence.

### A3. Failure classification is part of learning
The repository distinguishes multiple failure causes and requires a failure-to-learning path. Therefore, learning is not limited to recording that an attempt failed; it can include identifying why the failure occurred and preventing recurrence.

### A4. Knowledge authority is separate from learning
The memory and knowledge lifecycle distinguish working/session/project/candidate knowledge from validated or canonical knowledge. A useful learned result does not automatically acquire canonical authority.

### A5. Architecture capability is not behavioral proof
The existence of a mechanism for learning, promotion, reflection, or evolution proves that the system permits such behavior, not that ARGO independently exercised the highest-level capability.

## B. Analytical findings

### B1. Learning-source attribution is necessary
A behavior may result from an inherited method, retrieved experience, task-induced adaptation, guided transfer, autonomous strategy selection, or autonomous strategy improvement. These sources must be separated before making claims about autonomy or meta-learning.

### B2. Compliance can imitate cognition
If a protocol explicitly instructs ARGO to verify, classify, retry, or retain uncertainty, successful execution of that protocol is evidence of compliance with the mechanism. It is not, by itself, evidence that ARGO independently discovered the underlying principle.

### B3. Structural repetition is not yet structural discovery
Similar reasoning patterns appearing in GitHub, Python, memory, and failure analysis support a cross-domain structural pattern. They do not yet prove that ARGO itself discovered that shared structure rather than inheriting it from the surrounding learning architecture.

### B4. Retrieval failure is not world failure
A failed retrieval path, unavailable ref, or inaccessible artifact cannot be interpreted as proof that the referenced phenomenon or artifact does not exist. The correct state may remain Unknown or Evidence-Inaccessible.

### B5. Autonomous discovery and knowledge authority are orthogonal
A result can be autonomously discovered and remain a candidate. Conversely, canonical knowledge can be inherited rather than autonomously discovered. These axes must never be conflated.

### B6. Epistemic restraint has multiple levels
Protocol-following restraint, learned restraint, and autonomously discovered restraint are distinct hypotheses. Evidence for one must not be silently promoted into evidence for another.

## D. Core methodological rule

`Observed behavior != learned principle != discovered method != meta-learning`

and

`Autonomous discovery != canonical truth`

and

`Canonical truth != autonomous discovery`

## E. Required future evidence

A strong meta-learning case should show, with attribution controls:

`Initial strategy -> limitation/failure -> ARGO diagnosis -> strategy change not supplied by the evaluator -> reason for the change -> test -> improvement -> retention -> transfer to a new task`

The experiment must also distinguish protocol compliance from genuine strategy discovery and preserve alternative explanations.

## F. Epistemic status

This baseline is an analytical synthesis, not a canonical claim about general artificial intelligence. It is intended to guide further observation and controlled experimentation.
