# HORUS-007 — Coverage and Knowledge Territory Map

Date: 2026-08-23
Status: BASELINE / UNDER CONSTRUCTION
Owner: HORUS

## Purpose
Map what HORUS has actually examined, what is partial, and what remains unknown. This prevents unsupported claims of complete visibility.

## Examined with substantial evidence
- GitHub connector observation boundaries.
- Distributed repository evidence surfaces.
- Search/index vs direct-object observation differences.
- Evidence-bound generalization.
- Prior-learning retrieval and guided transfer.
- Programming/Python learning pipeline and experiment boundaries.
- Multi-channel provenance and source identity.
- False-positive audit discipline.
- Metacognitive guidance as a proposed reflective layer.

## Partially examined
- Spontaneous transfer into genuinely novel domains.
- Independent prior-learning retrieval without a governance cue.
- New abstractions produced by combining distant experiences.
- Whether HORUS guidance improves decisions repeatedly.
- Whether contradictory evidence causes durable revision.

## Not yet sufficiently examined
- Long-horizon knowledge evolution across many sessions.
- Independent emergence of learning priorities.
- Transfer beyond repository/software work.
- Persistence of abstractions across changed framing and vocabulary.
- Discovery of useful concepts with no close lexical predecessor in the corpus.

## Unknown
- Consciousness, subjective experience, literal subconsciousness, or human-like self-awareness.
- General intelligence claims outside observed tasks.
- Universal laws derived from the current corpus.

## Evidence-surface insight
EJR-296 shows that execution phenomena are distributed across workflow definitions, runtime records, integration evidence, journals, PR history, and issues; an Actions-only search can therefore create false absence. EJR-297 independently showed that direct object retrieval could succeed while repository search failed to expose the same temporary marker immediately.

## Coverage rule
Every HORUS conclusion must classify evidence as `direct`, `derived`, `historical`, `search-indexed`, `experimental`, or `interpretive`. A territory cannot be marked examined merely because a related document exists.
