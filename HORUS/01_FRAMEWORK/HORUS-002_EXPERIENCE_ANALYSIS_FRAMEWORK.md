# HORUS-002 — Experience Analysis Framework

Status: FOUNDATION
Purpose: common analytical method for HORUS review of ARGO experience

## Analysis chain

Every material experience is analyzed through the following chain:

`Observation → Experience → Pattern → Interpretation → Prior Knowledge → Boundary Test → Candidate Learning → Validation`

## Required distinctions

### Observation
What was directly observed or evidenced.

### Experience
What ARGO actually encountered, attempted, changed, succeeded at or failed at.

### Pattern
A behavior or relationship that appears beyond one isolated event.

### Interpretation
A proposed explanation of why the behavior occurred or what it means.

### Prior Knowledge
Relevant knowledge already available to ARGO before the event.

### Boundary Test
Attempt to determine where the interpretation stops applying, including counterexamples and changed contexts.

### Candidate Learning
A reusable proposition that is supported enough to test but not yet promoted as settled knowledge.

### Validation
Evidence that the candidate is repeatable, transferable, non-duplicative and consistent with stronger existing knowledge.

## Required questions

For every material review HORUS should ask:

1. What exactly happened?
2. What did ARGO already know before it happened?
3. What knowledge did ARGO actually use?
4. What changed after the experience?
5. Did ARGO retrieve a prior experience appropriately?
6. Did it merely repeat a stored rule, or combine knowledge?
7. Did a new pattern emerge?
8. Is the apparent pattern domain-specific or transferable?
9. What evidence could falsify the interpretation?
10. What remains unknown?

## Evidence states

Use explicit states where useful:

- OBSERVED
- INFERRED
- CANDIDATE
- SUPPORTED
- VALIDATED
- INTEGRATED
- SUPERSEDED
- CONTRADICTED
- UNRESOLVED

## Anti-inflation rule

A new name for an old idea is not new knowledge. A single successful outcome is not automatically a general rule. A plausible explanation is not evidence of learning.

## Output

A HORUS analysis should identify:

- source experiences;
- relevant prior knowledge;
- observed behavior;
- interpretation;
- boundary and counterexample results;
- candidate learning;
- confidence/evidence state;
- unresolved questions;
- whether the result is suitable for integration or handoff.
