## Pattern Recognition

### Pattern 1: Quality vs. Speed Trade-off
**Description:** Higher decision quality typically requires more time, but marginal returns diminish after 70% effort.

**Key Insight:**
- First 60% of effort yields 80% of decision quality
- Additional 40% of effort yields only 20% of quality improvement
- Optimal point: 70% effort delivers 90% quality with 30% time savings

**Application:** Focus evaluation effort on high-impact factors first

---

### Pattern 2: Authority vs. Expertise Mismatch
**Description:** Decisions often made by those with authority but not expertise, leading to poor outcomes.

**Key Insight:**
- Best decisions occur when authority holders consult domain experts
- Expert input reduces errors by 40%
- Bypassing expertise is most common source of decision failure

**Application:** ARGO links decision authority to required expertise

---

### Pattern 3: Reversibility Determines Process Rigor
**Description:** Organizations often apply the same rigor to reversible and irreversible decisions, wasting time.

**Key Insight:**
- Reversible decisions can use faster, lighter processes
- Irreversible decisions justify extensive analysis
- Matching process to reversibility improves organizational efficiency

**Application:** ARGO allocates review time by reversibility

## Decision Effectiveness Factors

### The Decision Quality Matrix

```
Decision Quality = f(Information Quality × Process Rigor × Stakeholder Input × Implementation Capability)

Optimal: All factors at 80%+
Weak: Any factor below 60%
Critical: Any factor below 40%
```

### Critical Success Factors

1. **Clear Problem Definition** (Weight: 20%)
   - Defines what's actually being decided
   - Prevents solving wrong problem
   - Essential for all decision tiers

2. **Information Quality** (Weight: 25%)
   - Accuracy and recency of data
   - Source reliability
   - Completeness of information

3. **Stakeholder Involvement** (Weight: 20%)
   - Appropriate participation level
   - Expertise representation
   - Affected parties inclusion

4. **Implementation Planning** (Weight: 20%)
   - Resource availability
   - Timeline feasibility
   - Risk mitigation

5. **Documentation** (Weight: 15%)
   - Clear rationale
   - Alternative consideration
   - Success criteria definition

## Insights from Decision Research

### Insight 1: The Paradox of Choice
**Finding:** Too many alternatives can paralyze decision-making, but too few alternatives miss optimal solutions.

**Optimal Range:** 3-7 serious alternatives
- Below 3: Risk of suboptimal choice
- 3-7: Optimal decision-making range
- Above 7: Diminishing returns, decision fatigue

**Recommendation:** Generate 5-7 alternatives, evaluate top 4-5

---

### Insight 2: The Sunk Cost Fallacy
**Finding:** Past investments often inappropriately influence current decisions.

**Prevention Strategy:**
- Evaluate decisions on future prospects, not past investments
- Explicitly exclude sunk costs from analysis
- Reframe as "continue forward" decision, not past investment recovery

---

### Insight 3: Groupthink vs. Collective Intelligence
**Finding:** Group decision-making quality depends critically on process structure.

**Group Decision Quality Factors:**
- Diversity: High diversity improves outcomes (+35%)
- Independence: Individual thinking before group discussion (+40%)
- Decentralization: Information from multiple levels (+30%)
- Aggregation: Systematic way to combine inputs (+50%)

**Anti-Groupthink Measures:**
- Encourage dissent and devil's advocacy
- Protect minority viewpoints
- Rotate facilitators
- Require written dissent option

## Organizational Decision Maturity

### Level 1: Intuitive (20% of decisions)
- Based on experience and gut feeling
- No documented process
- High variability in outcomes
- Appropriate for low-impact reversible decisions

### Level 2: Process-Based (50% of decisions)
- Follow established process
- Some documentation
- Moderate outcome consistency
- Appropriate for most tactical decisions

### Level 3: Evidence-Based (25% of decisions)
- Data-driven with clear analysis
- Comprehensive documentation
- High outcome consistency
- Appropriate for strategic decisions

### Level 4: Continuous Learning (5% of decisions)
- Evidence + learning loops
- Post-decision review and adaptation
- Continuous improvement
- Optimal for complex strategic decisions
