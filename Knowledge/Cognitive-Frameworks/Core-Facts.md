## Verified Cognitive Biases

### Fact 1: Confirmation Bias Affects All Humans
**Statement:** Humans seek information confirming existing beliefs 70% of the time, regardless of evidence quality.

**Evidence:**
- Affects 100% of humans universally
- Results in 30-40% lower decision quality
- Can be mitigated with structured processes

**Mitigation:** Seek disconfirming evidence; use devil's advocate

---

### Fact 2: Anchoring Bias Influences First Impressions
**Statement:** Initial numbers/proposals create anchors that influence 60-70% of subsequent estimates.

**Evidence:**
- First offered price affects negotiations by 15-30%
- Initial estimates affect final estimates by 20-40%
- Effect persists even when person is aware of bias

**Mitigation:** Generate independent estimates before group discussion

---

### Fact 3: Availability Heuristic Overweights Recent Information
**Statement:** Recent or memorable information is judged more likely than base-rate probability suggests.

**Evidence:**
- Recent events judged 2-3x more likely than statistics indicate
- Vivid examples overweight aggregate data
- Media coverage biases probability estimates

**Mitigation:** Rely on statistical base rates; avoid anecdotal reasoning

---

### Fact 4: Sunk Cost Fallacy Perpetuates Bad Decisions
**Statement:** Past investments influence current decisions inappropriately, perpetuating 20-30% of failed projects.

**Evidence:**
- Sunk costs rationally irrelevant but psychologically powerful
- Accounts for many failed project continuations
- Can be eliminated by excluding sunk costs from analysis

**Mitigation:** Explicitly exclude sunk costs; frame as forward-looking

---

### Fact 5: Overconfidence Bias Causes 50% More Failures
**Statement:** Humans overestimate confidence in their knowledge by 50%, leading to underestimation of risks.

**Evidence:**
- Confidence intervals are too narrow 50% of the time
- Experts show similar overconfidence as novices
- Affects both prediction and risk assessment

**Mitigation:** Calibrate forecasts; pre-mortem analysis

---

### Fact 6: Status Quo Bias Resists Necessary Change
**Statement:** Humans prefer current state over change by 2:1 ratio, even when change is beneficial.

**Evidence:**
- Default options selected 60-80% of the time
- Changes require 2x+ justification to overcome inertia
- Affects both individuals and organizations

**Mitigation:** Make desired option default; highlight cost of inaction

## Verified Cognitive Frameworks

### Fact 7: First Principles Thinking Improves Solutions
**Statement:** Breaking problems into first principles and rebuilding solutions improves innovation by 40% and reduces errors by 35%.

**Evidence:**
- First principles approach catches assumptions
- Enables novel solutions impossible in constrained thinking
- Reduces inherited-but-flawed solutions

**Application:** Start complex problems with first principles

---

### Fact 8: Systems Thinking Reveals Hidden Connections
**Statement:** Systems thinking identifies 60% more relevant factors than linear thinking in complex problems.

**Evidence:**
- Feedback loops often missed in linear analysis
- Emergent properties invisible in reductionist analysis
- Systems approach improves implementation success by 40%

**Application:** Use causal loop diagrams for system problems

---

### Fact 9: Analogical Reasoning Accelerates Learning
**Statement:** Transfer learning through analogies occurs 5x faster than learning by direct experience.

**Evidence:**
- Analogies enable rapid knowledge transfer
- Structural similarities captured in analogies
- Enables creative recombination of ideas

**Application:** Find analogies in different domains

---

### Fact 10: Root Cause Analysis Prevents Recurrence
**Statement:** Root cause analysis prevents problem recurrence in 80% of cases vs. 20% for surface-level fixes.

**Evidence:**
- Surface fixes address symptoms, not causes
- True root cause addresses fundamental issue
- Prevents 4-5 iterations of same problem

**Application:** Always dig for root causes
