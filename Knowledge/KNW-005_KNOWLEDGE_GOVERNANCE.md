# Purpose

This document defines how knowledge is governed throughout ARGO KOP while preserving the distinction between platform knowledge and knowledge belonging to users, projects and deployments.

# Governance Principle

**Knowledge is governed by scope, evidence and authority.**

A knowledge item may be useful without being canonical, and may be validated within a local context without becoming platform-wide knowledge.

# Knowledge Domains

## Platform Knowledge

Knowledge that is intentionally accepted as part of ARGO KOP's governed platform understanding.

## User / Project / Deployment Knowledge

Knowledge derived from or belonging to a particular user, project, team or deployment. It remains contextual unless explicitly promoted.

## Shared Candidate Knowledge

Knowledge that has evidence of broader applicability but remains a candidate until cross-context validation and applicable authorization are complete.

# Knowledge Object Requirements

Every governed knowledge object should preserve, as applicable:

- unique identity;
- owner;
- scope/domain;
- source/provenance;
- evidence state;
- classification;
- current status;
- relationships;
- version;
- review history;
- authority requirement.

# Authority and Status

The following states are distinct:

- `CANDIDATE` — proposed knowledge not yet validated;
- `VALIDATED` — supported within the declared scope;
- `AUTHORIZED` — approved for the applicable governed use;
- `CANONICAL` — published as platform knowledge under repository authority;
- `HOLD` — insufficient or conflicting evidence;
- `REJECTED` — reviewed and not accepted.

`VALIDATED` does not automatically mean `CANONICAL`.

# Cross-Domain Promotion

User, project or deployment knowledge may be proposed for broader reuse only after:

1. source and scope identification;
2. evidence review;
3. validation appropriate to the claim;
4. broader applicability assessment;
5. contradiction and alternative review;
6. privacy/confidentiality and deployment-specificity review where applicable;
7. impact analysis;
8. required authorization;
9. provenance and promotion record.

A repeated lesson is not automatically a platform rule.

Knowledge generated by an external AI model is not canonical merely because the model is trusted or the output is persuasive.

# Knowledge Modification

Material knowledge updates require, as applicable:

Repository Review

Architecture Validation

Governance Compliance

Evidence / Provenance Update

Relationship Review

Version Review

Folder Status Update

Downstream Impact Review

# Repository Rule

Repository knowledge is authoritative within its declared platform scope.

Conversation context, working memory and user/project knowledge may generate candidates but shall not silently replace canonical platform knowledge.

Platform knowledge shall not silently overwrite user-owned or project-owned knowledge merely because the platform version is newer.

# Error and Contradiction Learning

When knowledge changes because an error or contradiction was discovered, preserve:

- previous interpretation;
- contradicting evidence;
- identified cause of mismatch where known;
- corrected interpretation or candidate;
- affected relationships;
- validation performed;
- authority required;
- final disposition;
- memory/knowledge domain affected;
- any cross-domain promotion.

# Reviewability

These governance rules are themselves reviewable. If a rule is shown to be incorrect, contradictory or unnecessarily complex, it may be revised through the applicable governance process.
